03Section 1 · The problem

Why the obvious solutions fall short

ChatGPT  ·  RAG

Retrieves passages and fabricates the connection between them. Sounds confident even when the corpus is silent. No way to audit which document supports which claim.

Classical knowledge graphs

Require rigid, hand-curated schemas. Cannot express that the corpus simply has no answer. Building one for a new domain takes months.

What's missing is a system that reasons across documents, cites every step, and is honest about absence of evidence.

Section 1 · Approach04 / 22
05Section 1 · The unit of knowledge

The infon, stored as a cassette

An infon is a typed quadruple

⟨ predicate,  subject,  object  ;  polarity ⟩

Example, extracted from a 2024 LG press release:

⟨ supplies, LG_Energy_Solution, solid_state_cells ; +1 ⟩

Every infon is grounded to its source: document hash, byte offset, ingestion timestamp.

A cassette is one document

Stored as a single immutable .inf file: content-addressed, append-only, S3-native (or any object store, or local disk).

  • Time-travel. Every ingest creates a new snapshot. Old views remain queryable forever.
  • Schema migration without reingestion. Change the ontology, rewrite the index in seconds — 62× faster than re-extraction.
  • Idempotent. Re-running an ingest costs nothing.

07Section 1 · Calibrated uncertainty

"I don't know" as a first-class output

Every verdict is a four-part mass over three outcomes:

  • Supports — evidence backs the claim.
  • Refutes — evidence contradicts the claim.
  • θ (theta) — the corpus doesn't speak to it.

When the corpus has no relevant evidence, θ rises toward 1.0. The Analyst is constrained to tell the user rather than guess.

Mass is constructed via Dempster–Shafer combination, not a learned softmax — calibration is structural, not trained.

Section 1 · Approach07 / 22
08Section 1 · Multi-hop reasoning

Connecting facts across documents

Single-hop: "Does company A supply component X?" → answered from one document.

Multi-hop: "Does company A supply X through any supplier?" → answered by chaining facts across documents, each edge cited.

  • Most useful Mobis questions are multi-hop — partners of partners, supplier of a competitor's component, what changed since last review.
  • The reasoner uses guided search (MCTS) with a small frozen graph neural net as the chain scorer — it can find chains that flat retrieval misses entirely.
Section 1 · Approach08 / 22
09Section 1 · The Analyst

Conversational layer with hard guardrails

Your analysts never write queries. They ask in plain language. The system constrains itself.

How it works

The Analyst is a thin agent over the cassette store. It calls fixed tools — ask, connect, any_of — and returns a written answer with a citation panel. It cannot fabricate a chain — it can only report what the graph returned.

Cross-session memory: prior findings persist; the Analyst checks them at the start of every session.

Built-in non-negotiables

  • Never returns an answer without citing the sources that produced it.
  • When θ > 0.7, explicitly says "the corpus does not answer this."
  • Runs extraction_report() on any newly-ingested store before answering.
  • These are prompt-enforced constraints, not soft suggestions.

Top 3 benefits for Hyundai Mobis

Avoid duplication, time-travel, says "I don't know"

11Section 2 · Benefit 1

Reduce duplicated research — your highest priority

The problem

The same question is researched repeatedly across teams. Reports sit unread. Institutional knowledge is buried inside PPTs and PDFs that no one knows to look for.

The capability

A single graph traversal returns every fact on a topic, with every source document grouped. A fact cited in ten reports is stored once and returned with all ten citations.

Example query

"Show all internal knowledge on solid-state electrolyte suppliers, grouped by author team and date."

What changes

Analysts find existing knowledge before commissioning new work. Cross-team duplication becomes visible and measurable — not just a complaint.

KPI hook

Queries answered from internal knowledge vs queries that prompted new external research.

Section 2 · Benefits11 / 22
12Section 2 · Benefit 2

Track how knowledge evolves over time

The problem. Internal reports are written as snapshots. There's no easy way to ask "what did we say about X last year, and how has our view changed?"

The capability. Every ingest is an immutable snapshot. Nothing is ever rewritten. Queries can be scoped to any point in time.

  • What did internal documents say about Toyota's solid-state timeline in 2023 vs 2025?
  • Show the trajectory of our cost estimate for LiDAR over 18 months.

Time-travel is native to the cassette store. Schema migrations preserve historical queries — old answers stay correct.

Section 2 · Benefits12 / 22
13Section 2 · Benefit 3

Answers you can trust

Three trust mechanisms, one slide. They all answer the same governance question — can a decision-maker trust a result this system returns?

Every answer cites its sources

Each infon carries document hash, byte offset, ingestion timestamp. The Analyst is prompt-constrained never to output a verdict without citing the contributing sources. Click → opens the exact passage.

The system says "I don't know"

When θ > 0.7, the answer is "the corpus does not contain a verified answer." No fabrication on absent evidence — the abstention is structural, not learned.

Contradictions surface automatically

The polarity channel (+1 affirmed / −1 negated) lets the system find disagreements across documents. Pre-decision risk review becomes a one-line query.

Citations, abstention, contradictions — these are not features bolted on to make the system "responsible." They are structural properties of how infon represents knowledge.

16Section 3 · Week 1

What week 1 actually looks like

Pre-engagement (before week 1 starts)

  • Sample corpus delivered — 50–100 representative Korean documents.
  • Mobis internal glossary or taxonomy shared as a starting point.
  • PoC success criteria and KPI targets agreed in writing.

Days 1–2

  • Ontology co-design workshop with Mobis domain experts — anchor types (actors, relations, features), connective predicates, hierarchies. Seeded by your glossary; refined live against the sample corpus.
  • Korean-capable SPLADE variant swapped in and validated on the sample.
  • Document preprocessor running on Mobis formats (PPTX, PDF, DOCX, XLSX).
  • Ontology v1 implemented as the infon schema; ready for first ingest.

Days 3–4

  • First ingest of the sample corpus.
  • extraction_report() surfaces coverage gaps automatically.
  • Schema patch + re-ingest of failed cases.

Day 5

  • First internal demo: a hero query on real Mobis content, with citations.
The iteration cadence for the rest of the PoC: ingest → report → refine → re-ingest.

19Section 3 · Risks

Risks and mitigations

RiskMitigation
Bundled SPLADE-tiny is English-trained; Korean documents need a different encoder. Week-1 swap to a multilingual or Korean-trained SPLADE variant (KoSPLADE / mSPLADE); validate anchor coverage on a Korean sample before scaling ingest.
Korean tokenisation / morphological variation degrades anchor matching. Korean-aware tokenizer in the encoder; anchor vocabulary seeded from the Mobis Korean glossary; verify with extraction_report().
Schema v1 extracts poorly on real Mobis content. Run extraction_report() on the week-1 sample; iterate before scaling ingest.
Security review blocks document access. Start week 1 with a "safe" curated subset already agreed pre-engagement.
KPI targets disputed at week 10. Agree targets in writing in week 1; measurement method documented in the SoW.
