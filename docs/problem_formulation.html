Text is a hypergraph.

A sketch of what we are really trying to solve — and the first piece of evidence that a GNN with logic in the aggregators can actually do it.

→ Read ~8 min · 8 slides
§1 · The question

How does a machine know
what a document claims?

Today — it either reads it opaquely or stores it flatly. Neither lets you ask "why".

What a machine reads
▁Toyota▁invests▁in▁solid-state▁batteries▁in▁Japan.

→ token sequence → 768-d blob
No identity. No polarity. No tense.
No provenance. No ignorance.
?
What a human means

An infon — a triple with tense, polarity, confidence, provenance.

§2 · Why existing approaches fail

Three tools. None fit.

The field has spent the decade swinging between opaque and brittle and bottomless. Knowledge graphs lose context. LLMs lose inspection. RAG loses calibration. And none of them can say "I don't know" in a way you can trust.

Option A Knowledge
graphs

Structured claims with typed relations. Query by traversal. Symbolic and inspectable.

But triples strip tense, polarity, source, hedging. "Toyota invested" and "Toyota may invest" collapse to the same edge.

Option B Language
models

Any text in, any answer out. Fluent. Reads context implicitly.

Can't tell you which claims were considered. Averages conflict into confident-sounding prose. Hallucinates as readily as it recalls.

Our bet Infon
hypergraph + GNN

Text → infons → typed hypergraph → GNN whose aggregators are logic. Output is a belief mass, not a guess.

Requires a seed schema. Buys back inspection, calibration, and the ability to say θ — "I don't know."

§3 · The insight

The edge type is
the logical connective.

A graph neural network has one aggregator per edge type. We choose those aggregators so the forward pass is the logic.

Spokes of a triple: AND (all must hold). Temporal edges: IF–THEN (premise gates conclusion). Spatial edges: IST (context gates content). A step of message passing is a step of inference.

→ after Barwise (infons) and Hayes (IKL)

§6 · Open problems

Three questions
this corpus does not answer.

One small scenario proves the architecture can compile. It does not prove anything about how the graph behaves at scale — across corpora, across domains, across shared ontology.

01.

Can one schema reason across two domains?

"invests" means something in autos and something else in biotech. Same anchor name — different semantic neighbourhood. Share or split?

02.

Does the propagation survive bidirectional?

Spoke edges are directed. Only the subject sends messages into the infon. Object-side interventions vanish — exposed by our confounder test.

03.

Does the recipe generalise?

The self-discovery pipeline assumed clean three-sentence chronology. Does it hold up on noisy news text, scientific abstracts, legal prose?
