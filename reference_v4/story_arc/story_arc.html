From Flat Text to Simulation

Your product data, market reports, and news feeds already contain the answers.

The question is whether your tools can reach them.

Today

You Have Documents

Product specs. Competitor reports. News feeds. Regulatory filings. Thousands of documents across brands and markets.

Your AI reads them as flat text — one chunk at a time.

The Problem

Search Gives You Topic Islands

Ask "who competes with the Camry?" and text search finds the Toyota island — everything that mentions the Camry.

The competitors are on other islands. Nothing connects them.

The Gap

Similar Words ≠ Real Relationship

The Camry and the Accord use similar words in their spec sheets. Text search puts them near each other.

But "near each other" isn't the same as "competes with." Proximity is not a relationship.

The Transformation

Name the Entities. Label the Relationships.

The clusters dissolve. Each dot gets a type — brand, model, variant, feature, market. Labeled connections draw in between them.

The link that says "competes with" crosses island boundaries. It was always there in the data — text search just couldn't see it.

The Unlock

Same Question. Follow the Links.

"Who competes with the Camry?" Follow the "competes with" links: Accord, Sonata, Malibu, Elantra...

Text search found 6. The connected data found 185. Same data, same AI.

One hop across a labeled connection — that's the entire difference.

More Unlocks

Questions That Were Impossible Before

"Compare the Camry and the Accord" — pull both feature sets, compute the difference. 87 shared, 12 Camry-only, 8 Accord-only.

"Where does Toyota sell that BMW doesn't?" — follow "sold in" links for both, subtract. ASEAN, India, Mexico, Africa.

"Which brand has the most models?" — count across all 932 models. BMW: 85. Honda: 55. Audi: 42.

Layer 2

Connect the News Feed

94,000 market signals — news, patents, regulatory changes — each linked to the products, brands, and features they're about.

"Is Toyota or Tesla trending more positively?" — count positive vs negative signals. Toyota: +6.08%. Tesla: +8.99%.

"What happened after the DoE loan program?" — follow the timeline links. Event by event, in order.

The Signal

Every Signal Connects to Everything It Touches

A signal isn't a loose headline. It's a hub — linked to the actor who emitted it, the products it's about, the features it affects, and the documents it came from.

Temporal NEXT chains link signals per product by date — 47K chains that turn scattered news into ordered timelines.

One signal, six typed connections. That's what makes "what happened after X" answerable.

Processing

Separating Signal from Noise

94K raw signals include duplicates, low-quality sources, and noise. MDUS scoring ranks each signal by source reliability, how many products it touches, and how large the impact is. Duplicates are collapsed.

PELT regime detection scans the timeline per product and finds the turning points — when did things shift from stable to disruption? From disruption to growth?

Raw feeds become ranked intelligence with annotated time periods. That's what the tools actually search.

Layer 3

Now Simulate

Make a copy of the connected data. Remove a product. Watch what fills the gap. Measure the impact on competitors, markets, features.

"What if Toyota discontinues the Camry?" — test 60 scenarios, score each against your goals, pick the best response.

That's not a report. That's a planning tool.

Layer 4

Don't Guess. Combine Evidence.

Four independent sources evaluate each piece of evidence: NLI classification, lexical overlap, temporal recency, source agreement.

Dempster-Shafer theory combines them into a single belief mass — not a probability, but an interval: how much supports, how much refutes, how much is unknown.

When evidence conflicts, the system reports uncertainty — it doesn't force a guess.

Layer 5

Search the Graph Like AlphaGo Searches Moves

Monte Carlo Tree Search explores the knowledge graph: SELECT the most promising path, EXPAND via NEXT edges and shared anchors, EVALUATE with NLI, BACKPROPAGATE belief.

Multi-hop claims that no single document answers — the tree search chains facts across documents and timelines.

Flat retrieval finds 5 docs, says "supports." MCTS finds the refuting branch too — calibrated belief, not overconfidence.

The Journey

Five Layers. Each One Unlocks the Next.

Text → lookups work, relationships don't.

Connected data → competitors, comparisons, market gaps, sentiment, timelines.

Belief calculus → calibrated verdicts from conflicting evidence, not forced guesses.

Graph reasoning → multi-hop chains, tree search, what-if simulation.

The answers were always in the data.

You just needed the right connections.
